# M-KF1 — Developer evaluation

## Evaluation frame

This is a visual-route assessment, not an implementation approval. The comparison uses the same copy, the same product truth, the same four keyframes, Chillax for human information, IBM Plex Mono for technical evidence, and the existing High-contrast civic color roles.

The target operator is not a generic enterprise sponsor. It is the person who must resolve a real exception across several financing parties and be able to defend the result later.

## A — Civic Ledger

### What works

- The category statement wins immediately because nothing competes with it.
- Wide rules and generous paper make the stable receivable legible without decoration.
- Builder truth is easy to scan and the light-to-dark proof transition is calm and credible.
- The system degrades well to mobile and grayscale.

### What weakens it

- Its restraint is close to the current production baseline.
- The credit / operations protagonist is present in the content more than in the composition.
- As real exception data grows, the system may become a conventional institutional dashboard unless a stronger operating grammar is introduced later.

### Implementation view

Low responsive risk. The layout can be built with conventional CSS Grid and simple breakpoint changes. Estimated cost for the four public keyframes: 4–6 frontend days, including visual-delta correction and accessibility checks.

## B — Operational Dispatch

### What works

- The operational rail makes truth, owner, deadline, and proof feel like one continuous case file.
- Transformation facts and builder capability boundaries can accept real data without changing the visual language.
- The route naturally connects public narrative, `/developers`, and product surfaces while allowing different density per audience.
- The proof register feels causally related to the light interface rather than appended to it.
- It stays credible in grayscale because hierarchy is carried by position, rule weight, and scale—not color alone.

### What weakens it

- The rail consumes valuable width and cannot survive unchanged at 390 px.
- Operational credibility could become visual density if every future field is promoted to the first level.
- The hero needs careful copy-length control so the system does not look like Workspace before the visitor understands the category.

### Implementation view

Medium responsive risk. The desktop rail becomes a compact top-level cue or disappears on mobile; this must be handled intentionally rather than merely stacked. Estimated cost: 6–8 frontend days.

## C — Evidence Cut

### What works

- It has the strongest unaided personality and the clearest visual tension.
- The receivable remains stable while protection becomes a single unmistakable displacement.
- The proof transition feels like the direct residue of the transformation.
- Large empty fields prevent the expressive typography from becoming general UI noise.

### What weakens it

- Crimson carries more narrative weight and therefore has less room left for precise conditional meaning.
- The dramatic cuts are harder to reconcile with dense developer and operations data.
- On mobile, the hierarchy needs bespoke composition per keyframe; simple stacking weakens the concept.
- It has the highest risk of reading as a campaign or editorial microsite instead of a durable product system.

### Implementation view

High responsive risk and the largest visual QA surface. Estimated cost: 8–11 frontend days, with more correction cycles around line breaks, overflow, and continuity between light and dark states.

## Recommendation boundary

Direction B is the developer recommendation because it best connects the chosen protagonist, public explanation, integration transparency, and future product data. This is a recommendation only. The owner must select A, B, or C before any production implementation or synthesis.
