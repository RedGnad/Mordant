# Shadow pilot discovery guide

Use this guide with 5–8 receivables platforms, factors, or multi-funder credit operators. Interview credit and operations practitioners first; include risk, legal, product, or technical stakeholders where they own part of the workflow.

Do not pitch features during the first pass. Ask for one recent concrete exception, follow its evidence, actors, handoffs, and timing, and separate observed facts from estimates.

## Twelve questions

1. Tell me about the most recent case where two obligations or claims could not both be satisfied as expected. What first made the team suspect a conflict?
2. Which systems, files, inboxes, or counterparties held the facts needed to understand that case?
3. Who decided that the situation was a real exception, and what evidence did they need before deciding?
4. How many internal and external parties became involved from detection through closure? What did each one own?
5. How was responsibility assigned, disputed, or transferred between those parties?
6. Which deadlines mattered, where were they recorded, and what happened when somebody did not respond?
7. Walk me through the manual exchanges required—messages, calls, documents, reconciliations, and approvals.
8. Where do errors, ambiguity, or delay create real cost today? Ask for operational impact rather than a hypothetical market size.
9. What proof must remain after the case closes, who reviews it, and in what form must it be exported?
10. Which parts of the process could a system recommend safely, and which decisions must remain human or legally reviewed?
11. What measurable result would make a shadow pilot worth continuing, and what outcome would make you stop it?
12. Which anonymized fields, representative events, policies, and historical cases could you share for a pilot without exposing confidential or regulated data?

## Interview record

Record the participant’s role and experience band, the example workflow, systems named, parties involved, timing estimates, failure costs, evidence requirements, shareable data boundary, pilot success condition, and explicit contradictions or unknowns.

Do not record borrower names, wallet keys, credentials, or unnecessary personal information. Agree how notes will be used and deleted before the interview begins.

## Synthesis after 5–8 interviews

Group evidence by recurring workflow, decision owner, source system, approval boundary, proof requirement, and measurable cost. Prioritize an onboarding or integration capability only when it removes a repeated obstacle in the selected pilot workflow; do not turn isolated feature requests into the product model.
